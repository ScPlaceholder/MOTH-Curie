Order matters

Start at 1∠0 and apply two moves, rotate by π/2 and conjugate (flip the phase), in each order. Then interfere each result with 1∠π/2.

your numbers

With the starting numbers: same two moves, opposite outcomes. Rotations alone commute; the flip is what makes order visible, except when the rotation is a half turn.

Maria Skłodowska-Curie, 1867–1934

Born in Warsaw, she left for Paris to study, because women could not enrol at a university at home. With Pierre Curie she found two new elements in tonnes of pitchblende, and named the first polonium, after a country that was not then on any map.

1903Nobel Prize in Physics, with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel, for the study of radioactivity
1911Nobel Prize in Chemistry, for the discovery of polonium and radium

The first woman to win a Nobel Prize, and still the only person to win one in two different sciences. Her notebooks are still radioactive; the library that keeps them stores them in lead-lined boxes.

She looked at something nobody could see and measured it until it could not be argued with. That is what this little microscope tries to do with amplitudes. While this card is open, the waves glow radium green.

Nothing in life is to be feared, it is only to be understood.attributed to Marie Curie
